Assessment summary

Important: BMI is a screening measure

BMI does not directly measure body fat percentage, muscle mass, bone density, fat distribution, metabolic health or overall fitness. Muscular people can have a higher BMI without high body fat. Consider BMI alongside other health information.

      Health Guide

      Nutrition

      Regular, balanced meals support steady energy.

      • Include vegetables and fruit daily
      • Choose whole grains where possible
      • Add a protein source to meals

      Movement

      Regular activity matters at every size.

      • Find activities you enjoy
      • Add strength work during the week
      • Break up long periods of sitting

      Sleep

      Consistent sleep supports mood and recovery.

      • Keep regular sleep and wake times
      • Limit screens before bed
      • Keep your room dark and cool

      Hydration

      Water needs vary with climate and activity.

      • Keep water within reach
      • Drink more in hot weather
      • Notice thirst and urine colour

      Healthy Habits

      Small routines tend to last longer than big changes.

      • Change one habit at a time
      • Track trends, not daily swings
      • Plan for busy days

      Professional Support

      A professional can look at the whole picture.

      • Book routine health checks
      • Discuss weight changes you did not intend
      • Ask a dietitian for tailored plans

      About BMI

      Adult BMI categories

      BMICategory
      Below 18.5Underweight
      18.5 – 24.9Healthy weight
      25.0 – 29.9Overweight
      30.0 and aboveObesity (Class I 30–34.9, II 35–39.9, III 40+)

      How it is calculated

      BMI = weight (kg) divided by height (m) squared. The formula is the same for all adults regardless of sex, though body composition and health interpretation can differ.

      For people under 18, BMI is read against age- and sex-specific growth percentiles, so adult categories do not apply.
